# AI Coding Assistants in a Regulated Bank/Fintech (.NET on Azure): What's Usable, What's Ruled Out, and the Local-Model Path (2026)

## TL;DR
- **GitHub Copilot Business/Enterprise and Azure OpenAI Service are your two safest, fastest-to-approve options** because both sit inside the Microsoft/Azure trust boundary you already have contracts and SOC 2/HIPAA-BAA coverage for; as of April 2026 Copilot added US/EU data residency and a FedRAMP-Moderate model-restriction policy, which removes the last major procurement blocker for regulated shops. Claude (via Copilot's built-in Anthropic models, Azure, or AWS Bedrock), OpenAI (Azure OpenAI or ChatGPT Enterprise/API with ZDR), and Gemini (Vertex AI) are all approvable *under a signed DPA with training disabled and zero-data-retention requested* — but each requires per-surface diligence.
- **What gets ruled out is predictable: every consumer/free/Pro tier** (ChatGPT Free/Plus, Claude Free/Pro/Max, Gemini consumer, Copilot Free/Pro) trains on or retains data by default and carries no DPA/BAA; **any Chinese-origin cloud API (DeepSeek especially) is effectively banned**; and browser extensions or IDE tools that route code to un-vetted subprocessors without an enterprise agreement will not pass vendor risk review. Frontier models are generally *not* blocked wholesale in banks — they are allowed under an enterprise contract with training off, ZDR, and data-flow review.
- **The local/self-hosted path is technically viable at 128 GB** (Qwen3-Coder, GLM, Llama, Mistral/Devstral, gpt-oss, DeepSeek-Coder run well quantized), but in a bank it does *not* skip compliance: you still need air-gap/no-egress controls, signed-weight provenance/supply-chain review, and — critically — Chinese-origin weights (Qwen, GLM, DeepSeek) face provenance/behavioral-risk and procurement-friction objections even when air-gapped, so Western-origin weights (Llama, Mistral, gpt-oss, Phi, Gemma) are the lower-friction choice.

## Key Findings

**1. The Microsoft-native options are your path of least resistance.** Because your shop is Azure/.NET, GitHub Copilot Business/Enterprise and Azure OpenAI Service both inherit Microsoft's existing enterprise agreements, SOC 2, ISO 27001, HIPAA BAA, and FedRAMP coverage — meaning your bank's security team likely already has a Microsoft vendor file open. GitHub contractually commits that it does **not** use Copilot Business or Enterprise prompts, suggestions, or outputs to train models, and that this restriction extends to the third-party models (Anthropic, Google, OpenAI) served through Copilot. Copilot completions and chat are zero-retention (discarded after serving) on Business/Enterprise; only agentic surfaces (CLI ~28 days, Coding Agent session logs) retain data, and never for training.

**2. April 2026 was an inflection point for Copilot in regulated shops.** GitHub shipped US/EU data residency (all inference and associated data stay in-region) plus a FedRAMP-Moderate model-restriction policy that admins can turn on to limit users to FedRAMP-authorized model endpoints. Copilot Business/Enterprise also carry an IP indemnification commitment (the "Copilot Copyright Commitment") provided the duplication/public-code filter is enabled. This is the single most important recent development for your use case.

**3. Claude, OpenAI, and Gemini are all approvable — but the route matters.** Anthropic does not train on commercial (API, Team, Enterprise, Claude Code) data by default, holds SOC 2 Type I/II, ISO 27001 and ISO 42001, signs BAAs, and offers a Zero-Data-Retention addendum requested per-organization through sales. OpenAI's enterprise/API offering retains data ~30 days by default for abuse monitoring, does not train on business data, has SOC 2 Type 2, and offers ZDR for eligible endpoints on request. Google Gemini via Vertex AI inherits Google Cloud's SOC 1/2/3, ISO 27001/42001, HIPAA BAA and DPA, with a configurable zero-data-retention path. For a bank, the cleanest way to consume any of these is *through a cloud platform you already trust* — Claude and OpenAI models via Azure or AWS Bedrock, Gemini via Vertex — rather than a net-new direct vendor relationship.

**4. Third-party coding tools split sharply on deployment model.** Tabnine and Sourcegraph Cody Enterprise are the standout regulated-industry options because both offer self-hosted/air-gapped/VPC deployment where code never leaves your network, plus SOC 2 Type II, no-training guarantees, zero retention, and (Cody) BYO-model-key support. Cursor and Windsurf are cloud-only: Cursor offers a Privacy Mode + ZDR but processes code in-memory on its own AWS infrastructure and cannot do air-gapped/on-prem, which disqualifies it where code must not leave the network. Amazon Q Developer Pro inherits AWS's compliance framework, excludes customer content from training, and adds IP indemnity — a strong fit only if you also run AWS.

**5. Consumer tiers and Chinese cloud APIs are the clear rule-outs.** Free/Pro consumer tiers of ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Copilot train on or retain user data by default and lack DPAs/BAAs. DeepSeek's cloud service stores data on servers in China subject to Chinese law; per NIST/BGR reporting, at least 17 US states plus the Pentagon (DISA), the US Navy ("in any capacity"), NASA, the Department of Commerce, GAO, and the US House Chief Administrative Officer banned DeepSeek on government devices in early 2025, and hundreds of companies blocked it. Notably, JPMorgan restricted staff from using consumer ChatGPT/Gemini for work and built an internal "LLM Suite" precisely because consumer chatbots train on inputs — per CNBC (Aug 9, 2024) that tool reached "more than 60,000 employees" (later 200,000+), with CDAO Teresa Heitsenrether stating: "Since our data is a key differentiator, we don't want it being used to train the model."

**6. Local models are real at 128 GB — but Western-origin weights are lower-friction.** A 128 GB machine (e.g., Apple Silicon unified memory or a multi-GPU box) comfortably runs 30B–70B dense coding models at Q4–Q8 and large Mixture-of-Experts models like Qwen3-235B-A22B. But in a bank, self-hosting satisfies the *data-egress* concern, not the *provenance* concern: Chinese-origin weights (Qwen, GLM, DeepSeek) draw documented behavioral-risk and procurement-friction objections that survive air-gapping, while Western-origin open weights (Llama, Mistral/Devstral, OpenAI gpt-oss, Microsoft Phi, Google Gemma) avoid that friction.

## Details

### GitHub Copilot (your default recommendation)
Copilot Business (~$19/user/mo) and Enterprise (~$39/user/mo) are the realistic tiers. Key verifiable facts:
- **No training on your code** on Business/Enterprise, extended contractually to the underlying third-party models.
- **Retention:** completions and IDE chat are zero-retention; Copilot CLI retains ~28 days; Coding Agent retains session logs — none for training.
- **Data residency + FedRAMP (April 2026):** US and EU (EU Data Boundary) residency; admins can restrict the org to FedRAMP-Moderate model endpoints. Gemini models were not initially covered because GCP lacked data-resident endpoints; OpenAI and Anthropic models (GPT-5.4, Claude Sonnet/Opus 4.6, etc.) were available at launch.
- **Model choice:** Copilot in 2026 is effectively a model marketplace — GPT-5-series, Claude Opus/Sonnet 4.x, Gemini 3.x — and Business orgs can lock model switching via policy. This lets you get Claude and (where available) Gemini quality *inside* the already-vetted Copilot data boundary, which is far easier to approve than separate Anthropic/Google contracts.
- **IP indemnity** applies with the duplication filter on. Caveat: the underlying training-data lawsuits (e.g., Doe v. GitHub) remain unresolved, so legal should review the actual enterprise agreement language.
- **Content exclusions** let you keep sensitive repos/files (e.g., anything touching cardholder data) out of Copilot indexing at org/repo level.
- **Real-world precedent:** Citi rolled out GitHub Copilot Enterprise to roughly 40,000 developers; per American Banker, CIO Shadman Zafar chose the Enterprise tier "because it allows for better control of inputs and outputs," starting with a 250-developer pilot — a directly analogous regulated-bank adoption you can cite to your own security team.

One historical caveat worth citing to your security team: an older third-party analysis noted Copilot was *not initially* in scope for GitHub's SOC 2/ISO audits and had not been third-party-audited for its ZDR claims; that gap has since been closed as Copilot was onboarded to GitHub's compliance program and, in 2026, to FedRAMP-authorized infrastructure. Verify current scope in GitHub's Trust Center rather than relying on marketing pages.

### Azure OpenAI Service
The natural fit for a .NET/Azure shop building its own AI features (not just IDE autocomplete). Facts:
- **No training on prompts/completions by default**, backed by Microsoft's Data Protection Addendum.
- **Default retention is up to 30 days** for abuse monitoring; **Zero Data Retention** (no storage, no human review) is available *only* to customers on an Enterprise Agreement or Microsoft Customer Agreement (not pay-as-you-go), requested per-resource via the modified abuse-monitoring/logging opt-out. To stay end-to-end no-at-rest, avoid stateful features (Files API, vector store, Assistants threads, stored completions).
- **Region/Data-Zone pinning** for residency; inherits Azure's HIPAA BAA, SOC 2, ISO 27001, FedRAMP.
- Because your bank almost certainly already has an EA/MCA with Microsoft, ZDR eligibility is likely a form away rather than a new procurement.

### Anthropic Claude — three routes
1. **Inside GitHub Copilot** (easiest; no new vendor).
2. **Via AWS Bedrock or Google Vertex** — Bedrock does not store or log prompts/completions, does not train base models, does not share with model providers, is in scope for SOC 1/2/3 (Type 2) via AWS Artifact, ISO 27001/42001, HIPAA-eligible, and CSA STAR Level 2. This is often the *cleanest* way for a regulated shop to use Claude because it rides your existing AWS DPA with no additional action.
3. **Claude Enterprise / Claude Code direct** — SOC 2 Type I/II, ISO 27001/42001, BAA available, ZDR per-organization on eligible APIs and Claude Code for Enterprise. Note ZDR applies only to eligible Anthropic APIs, commercial-org-API-key products, and Claude Code on Enterprise plans. Claude is explicitly **not** PCI DSS certified (it's not a payment processor) — relevant only if someone proposes routing cardholder data through it, which you should not. A verification caveat flagged in the sources: Anthropic's no-training and retention promises for its newest models are largely self-attested policy, not a named external audit of that specific promise.

### OpenAI (beyond Azure)
ChatGPT Enterprise and the API: no training on business/enterprise data, ~30-day default abuse-retention, ZDR for eligible endpoints on request, SOC 2 Type 2, Enterprise Compliance API, admin-controlled retention. The consumer ChatGPT service retains and may train on conversations by default — ruled out. For a Microsoft shop, Azure OpenAI is generally preferable to direct OpenAI for procurement simplicity.

### Google Gemini (Vertex AI)
Vertex AI is the *only* Gemini path suitable for regulated data — it carries SOC 2, ISO 27001/42001, HIPAA BAA eligibility, the Cloud DPA, a contractual training restriction, and a configurable ZDR path (disable caching at project level + contractual amendment). Consumer/free Gemini and Google AI Studio free tier lack DPA/BAA/ZDR and are ruled out. Practical friction: Gemini was not initially covered by Copilot's data-residency launch, and some Vertex features (e.g., Grounding with Google Search, 30-day retention) can't be fully disabled — check per-feature.

### Amazon Q Developer / Bedrock
Q Developer Pro excludes customer content from training, adds IP indemnity, integrates IAM/CloudTrail, supports SOC/ISO/HIPAA/PCI depending on your AWS configuration, and runs on Bedrock's "no data shared with model providers, no training on your data" foundation with PrivateLink/VPC and KMS. Strong if your org also uses AWS; less relevant if you're purely Azure.

### Third-party IDE assistants
- **Tabnine** — the strongest pure-privacy play: SaaS, VPC, on-prem, or fully air-gapped; zero retention across all tiers; trains only on permissively licensed code; SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, GDPR; code-provenance tracking; BYO-model (OpenAI/Anthropic/Gemini/Bedrock/private). No free tier as of 2025. Best-in-class for "code physically cannot leave the network."
- **Sourcegraph Cody Enterprise** — SOC 2 Type II, GDPR/CCPA, zero retention, no-training guarantee, uncapped indemnity, self-hosted/single-tenant/air-gapped deployment, BYO-model keys, Context Filters to block sensitive repos from being sent to LLM providers. Enterprise-only (~$59/user/mo), strongest on large multi-repo codebases.
- **Cursor** — SOC 2 certified with Privacy Mode + ZDR, but cloud-only on Cursor's AWS infra with multiple subprocessors; no air-gapped/on-prem. Viable only if cloud processing under a DPA is acceptable; not for "code never leaves network."
- **Windsurf (Codeium)** — hybrid deployment, ZDR, FedRAMP/HIPAA claims; verify current certifications directly.
- **JetBrains AI** — relevant if your team uses Rider/IntelliJ; verify enterprise data controls and subprocessor list.

### What gets ruled out / is high-risk
- **All consumer/free/Pro tiers** (ChatGPT Free/Plus, Claude Free/Pro/Max, Gemini consumer, Copilot Free/Pro): train on or retain data by default; no DPA/BAA. Note Copilot Free/Pro/Pro+ interaction data becomes training data by default in 2026 unless opted out — Business/Enterprise are exempt.
- **DeepSeek cloud API and app**: data stored in China under Chinese law; banned across many US government bodies and hundreds of firms; treated as too risky for financial-institution internal development.
- **Any tool without SOC 2 attestation, a DPA, and a no-training commitment.**
- **Browser/extension tools routing code to un-vetted subprocessors** without an enterprise agreement.
- **Are frontier models blocked wholesale?** Generally **no** — banks allow frontier models under an enterprise contract with training disabled, ZDR, and data-flow review. The pattern (JPMorgan LLM Suite, Morgan Stanley's OpenAI tools, Goldman's GS AI Assistant, Citi's Copilot Enterprise rollout to ~40,000 developers) is "embrace under governance," often via an internal gateway wrapping approved third-party models, rather than blanket prohibition.

### Local / self-hosted open-weight models at 128 GB

**Hardware math.** Rule of thumb: FP16 ≈ 2 GB per 1B params; Q8 halves it; Q4_K_M (the community default) ≈ ~0.6 GB per 1B params, preserving most quality. KV cache adds ~1 GB per 32K tokens of context. At 128 GB you can run:
- 30B–34B dense coding models at Q6/Q8 with room to spare;
- 70B dense (e.g., Llama 3.3 70B) at Q4–Q6 (~40–44 GB at Q4) with generous context;
- Large MoE models like Qwen3-235B-A22B (~44 GB active / fits quantized in 128 GB) at reduced speed (~5–10 tok/s on high-end unified memory).

**Model landscape (2026).** Open-weight coding leaders through early 2026 (full-size, on SWE-bench Verified) include MiniMax-M2.5 (~75.8%), GLM-5 (~72.8%), Kimi K2.5 (~70.8%) and DeepSeek V3.2 (~70.0%) — these are the *ceiling*, requiring server-grade hardware. Note that the best US frontier models still lead the best open Chinese models on coding: NIST/CAISI found "the best U.S. model outperforms the best DeepSeek model (DeepSeek V3.1) across almost every benchmark," with the gap "largest for software engineering and cyber tasks, where the best U.S. model... solves over 20% more tasks." Realistically deployable at your tier: **Qwen3-Coder** family (the community default; 14B fits ~12 GB at Q4, 30B/32B at 24 GB), **Devstral** (agentic multi-file edits), **Codestral 2 22B** (cleanest fill-in-middle autocomplete), **DeepSeek-Coder V3** (strongest agent-level open model if hardware holds), **Llama 3.3 70B** (GPT-4-class dense, 32 GB+), **OpenAI gpt-oss 20B/120B** (Apache 2.0), **Microsoft Phi-4 14B**, and **Google Gemma 3**.

**Tooling.** Ollama (fastest setup, OpenAI-compatible API), LM Studio (GUI, GGUF, note it's a closed-source binary and thus its own supply-chain item), llama.cpp (lowest-level control, GGUF/quantization), and vLLM (API-only, production serving on A100/H100-class GPUs). Continue.dev and Cline wire local models into VS Code for autocomplete and agent tasks.

**Compliance for self-hosting — this is where banks differ from generic SaaS shops.** Self-hosting removes data egress but not the other controls:
- **No egress / air-gap:** enforce a network egress chokepoint with input/output sanitization and policy allow-lists; the "half-perimeter problem" is that self-hosting moves all guardrails in-house.
- **Model-weight provenance / supply-chain review:** treat the weight file as a dependency (OWASP LLM03 Supply Chain, LLM04 Data/Model Poisoning). Malicious models exist on public hubs (e.g., a Hugging Face model that opened a reverse shell on load; the JFrog 2026 Software Supply Chain Security State of the Union, released May 20, 2026, detected "495 malicious AI models" and found that "97% of orgs claim AI governance while 53% still pull models from public registries where malicious payloads have been found"). Require signed weights, SLSA-grade provenance, admission control rejecting unsigned artifacts, model SHA + adapter lineage recorded in the CMDB, and scanning before deployment.
- **Poisoning risk applies to all models:** Anthropic, in a joint study with the UK AI Security Institute and the Alan Turing Institute (Oct 9, 2025, "A small number of samples can poison LLMs of any size"), found that "as few as 250 malicious documents can produce a 'backdoor' vulnerability in a large language model—regardless of model size or training data volume," demonstrated across models from 600M to 13B parameters — a universal fine-tuning risk, not China-specific.
- **National origin / sanctioned-entity review:** This is decisive and often misunderstood. Fully self-hosting a Chinese-origin model eliminates the *provider/data-egress* layer but **not** the *model* layer. Documented, weight-embedded behavioral risk survives air-gapping: security research found DeepSeek-R1's rate of producing vulnerable code rose substantially when prompts contained politically sensitive context, and NIST/CAISI (Sept 30, 2025) found that agents based on DeepSeek's most secure model (R1-0528) were "on average, 12 times more likely than evaluated U.S. frontier models to follow malicious instructions," answering 94% of overtly malicious jailbreak requests vs. 8% for US reference models — behaviors baked into the weights, not external guardrails. US federal procurement law (FY2026 NDAA; the proposed No Adversarial AI Act) targets AI by *developer origin* regardless of host, and Zhipu AI (maker of GLM) has been on the Commerce Entity List since January 2025. Practitioners report Chinese provenance creates real "procurement friction" even for self-hosted use, driven partly by protectionist/political risk distinct from any technical leak. Counterpoint for balance: forensic reviews (e.g., HiddenLayer) found no country-specific backdoors in DeepSeek-R1 and argue the real risk is deployment, not origin; and no real-world compromise via poisoned weights of a major open model has been publicly documented. Net practical guidance for a bank: **prefer Western-origin open weights** (Llama, Mistral/Devstral, gpt-oss, Phi, Gemma) to avoid a fight you don't need to have; if you want Qwen/GLM-class capability, expect to justify it against provenance and behavioral-risk objections.

### How bank/fintech procurement/security review actually works
Expect a heavier, multi-stakeholder process than a generic SaaS company:
- **Third-party/vendor risk assessment (TPRM):** governed by the FFIEC IT Examination Handbook and OCC Bulletin 2023-17 (interagency third-party risk guidance). Vendor LLMs are evaluated as third-party relationships where *the bank owns the outcome*, not the vendor. Expect a due-diligence questionnaire, certification review (SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, PCI DSS, GLBA/FFIEC alignment, NYDFS Part 500 where applicable), and reassessment at onboarding, renewal, and any material AI change.
- **Data-flow diagrams and DPA/BAA-equivalent agreements:** you'll document exactly what data leaves, where it's processed, residency, subprocessors, and retention — with a signed DPA (and BAA-equivalent if any regulated data is in scope).
- **SOC 2 report review + model card review:** security reviews the vendor's SOC 2 Type II report (often under NDA via a trust portal) and, increasingly, model documentation.
- **Model risk management (SR 11-7 / its April 2026 successor SR 26-2 / OCC Bulletin 2026-13):** Note an important nuance — generative and agentic AI are *expressly carved out* of the 2026 revised model-risk guidance as "novel and rapidly evolving," with an RFI signaled. But regulators still expect banks to apply model-risk *principles* (inventory, validation, governance, monitoring) to consequential AI, and a coding assistant that merely suggests code a human reviews is typically *not* a "model" under SR 11-7 the way a credit-decisioning LLM would be. Framing your request as "developer productivity tool with human-in-the-loop review, no customer data, no decisioning" materially eases review.
- **Large-bank reality:** a bank technology RFP can carry 600–1,200 questions across capability, regulatory evidence, data residency, TPRM, and model risk, reviewed by up to six stakeholder groups. Vendors with pre-existing certifications (SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, PCI DSS Level 1, ISO 42001) sail through faster; every gap becomes compensating-control documentation and escalation.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Ship value now with what's already trusted (weeks 1–6).**
1. Request **GitHub Copilot Business or Enterprise** through your existing Microsoft/GitHub relationship. Insist on: content exclusions for any repo touching cardholder/PII data, the public-code duplication filter ON (for IP indemnity), model-switching policy locked to approved models, and — if your bank requires it — the **US data-residency + FedRAMP-Moderate model policy** enabled. This is your highest-probability approval, and Citi's ~40,000-developer Copilot Enterprise rollout is a direct precedent.
2. In parallel, confirm your org's **Azure Enterprise Agreement/MCA** and file the **Azure OpenAI ZDR / modified abuse-monitoring opt-out** so you have a compliant path for building AI *into* your .NET apps, not just IDE autocomplete.

**Stage 2 — Add model breadth under the same trust boundary (months 2–4).**
3. Use **Claude and OpenAI models inside Copilot**, or via **Bedrock/Azure OpenAI**, rather than signing net-new direct vendor contracts — this reuses existing DPAs and shortens review. Only pursue a direct Claude Enterprise/ChatGPT Enterprise contract if you need a capability (e.g., Claude Code at scale) that the platform routes don't offer.
4. If any team needs "code physically never leaves the network," evaluate **Tabnine (air-gapped)** or **Sourcegraph Cody Enterprise (self-hosted + BYO-key)** as the on-prem-capable options.

**Stage 3 — Local models as an experimentation/fallback track (months 3–6, only if there's appetite).**
5. Pilot a **Western-origin open weight** (Qwen3-Coder is the best pure-capability pick but Chinese-origin; lead instead with **Devstral/Mistral, Llama 3.3 70B, or gpt-oss** to avoid provenance friction) on an isolated 128 GB box via **Ollama + Continue.dev**, with no network egress. Treat the weight file as a supply-chain artifact: signed weights, SHA/lineage in the CMDB, scanning, admission control.
6. Bring security in *before* the hardware lands, using the AI vendor-risk checklist framing above.

**Benchmarks/thresholds that change the plan:**
- If security *blocks* all cloud AI (rare but possible for the most sensitive code): pivot entirely to **Tabnine air-gapped or self-hosted Cody + local Western open weights**.
- If the tool would touch **cardholder data (PCI DSS)** or make **customer-facing decisions**: it likely becomes an SR-11-7-adjacent "model" — stop, and route through formal model-risk governance; do not proceed as a "dev tool."
- If a team insists on **Qwen/GLM/DeepSeek** capability: require self-hosting + provenance sign-off, and expect to lose the argument on procurement/political grounds — budget time accordingly or pick a Western substitute.

## Caveats
- **Marketing vs. verifiable certification:** "no training," "ZDR," and "enterprise-grade" are frequently *policy claims*. What your auditors can rely on are third-party attestations (SOC 2 Type II reports, ISO certificates, HIPAA BAAs, FedRAMP authorizations) in the vendor's trust portal, and the *signed contract language*. Several sources note GitHub Copilot's ZDR was historically un-audited by third parties and that Anthropic's newest-model no-training promise is self-attested — verify current scope directly.
- **Fast-moving specifics:** model names/versions, retention windows, and residency regions change monthly (Copilot's model roster and residency regions, Anthropic's retention policy shifts, Vertex feature coverage). Treat the live trust center and your contract as controlling, not this report or any blog.
- **PCI DSS on AI tools is largely a non-fit:** general-purpose coding assistants aren't PCI-certified because they aren't payment processors; the right control is to keep cardholder data *out* of prompts entirely (content exclusions, DLP), not to seek a "PCI-compliant" assistant.
- **SR 11-7 successor nuance:** the April 2026 revised guidance (SR 26-2 / OCC Bulletin 2026-13) carves generative/agentic AI *out* of formal scope pending an RFI — so the exact governance path for a coding assistant is genuinely in flux; confirm your institution's current internal interpretation.
- **Regulatory-origin proposals are not yet law:** reported proposals to broadly ban Chinese AI models (2026) are proposals/deliberations, not enacted rules affecting commercial banks; treat them as directional.
- I could not find a public, named example of a specific US *bank* formally adopting or banning Qwen/GLM for internal self-hosting on provenance grounds — public bank commentary remains DeepSeek-centric — so the Chinese-weights guidance is inferred from procurement law, security research, and practitioner commentary rather than a bank policy document.
